Spween ↔ Objective Bend: a scene is a way into a world

Spween supplies the shape of an encounter: passages, prose, conditions, choices, and effects in their authored order. Objective Bend supplies the things the encounter is about: state, behavior, composition, and the meaning of its small verbs. DelveTalk gives their execution an accountable place to happen. A scene is an authored interface to a persistent world. Its door, cupboard, repair counter, or appointment book can have several interfaces without acquiring several competing truths.

The join happens at Spween's handler boundary. Generated Scene source imports an explicit Kernel and an ordinary Objective Bend Handler. The handler declares State, initial, get_var, set_var, has, begin, and call. Reading a scene variable calls its accessor; assignment returns the handler's next state; membership consults that state. begin wraps it in a Step carrying acceptance, reason, state, and emissions. call receives that Step, a name, typed arguments, and the invocation context, and returns the next Step. The compiler checks this interface. Handler source, scene source, and their exact module assembly remain inspectable together; no ambient callback registry supplies hidden behavior.

This is a useful separation of authorship. Someone writes a warm, spare scene about repairing a mechanical moth. Someone else provides the moth's actual repair behavior. A third supplies a workshop policy. The scene author can choose what to explain, when to reveal a choice, and which behavior to invoke without copying the rules for repair or permission. The behavior author can improve a mechanism without rewriting every paragraph that presents it. Their interface is checked, and adopting a revision is explicit.

Order is the decisive detail. Consider this illustrative effect sequence, expressed in prose rather than executable syntax: increase tension; call align-wing; increase tension again; enter the passage whose choices ask whether the moth is ready. The call sees the first increase. The second increase sees the state returned by the call. Readiness is computed from the resulting state. Extracting calls into a later batch would give a different program. The lowering threads one evolving handler state through every effect, preserving calls between assignments and modifications rather than collecting them at the end.

That order is transactional. A handler can refuse an operation; a bound can be exceeded; a later call can discover that its assumption is false. The whole admitted turn then leaves the object unchanged, including earlier effects in the same sequence. The effect order determines computation, while admission determines whether its result becomes history. This deliberately differs from a host embedding that mutates some variables before reporting an error. Looking at a scene does not replay entry effects, and returning to a passage does not silently repeat effects declared once per session.

The language boundary remains specific. The handler profile supports Null, Boolean, signed i64, and text, with explicit comparison and overflow rules; floating-point execution and string ordering are outside this profile. A port cannot quietly substitute JavaScript arithmetic or convert every truthy value into a Boolean. A handler's richer State may contain typed recursive collections even though the scene's variable vocabulary is small. A pocket can therefore hold an actual changing collection, while a scene asks the modest question “has inventory brass-key?” Missing values and unsupported operations have defined outcomes rather than guesses supplied by the presenter.

Dynamic membership is especially important because it turns conditions into questions about play. Taking the brass key changes what a later guard observes; lending it changes who can carry it; placing it on a shelf makes it available somewhere. The membership accessor computes from authoritative state. It does not gain permission to rummage through arbitrary objects merely because its function is named has. When membership depends on a separately governed relation, the connection uses admitted observations or a transaction with that relation. Observation supplies facts, current law supplies permission, and copied descriptions supply neither.

Shared places use one placement relation rather than asking every room to maintain a second inventory. Rooms remain independently authored admission policies. A move can plan from current placement, ask the destination to admit it, and commit the resulting arrival atomically. A portable object's ownership, custody, location, and operating permissions remain different questions. A gift requires consent; possession does not rewrite the object's law. A gallery's exhibition list is another relation again: showing a lantern does not acquire it. Scenes give these distinctions a readable surface—“leave it here,” “offer it,” “follow the exhibit”—without collapsing them into decorative links.

A small port becomes useful quickly. The mechanical-moth scene needs a few variables, two repair calls, and a readiness question. A rain-naming scene combines an ordinary collection of names with a choice that opens a shared exhibition. A tea room borrows a mailbox module to display invitations and a consent module to decide whose notices it accepts. These are light adaptations of scene behavior to explicit modules, not rewrites of whole stories into object-management forms. Conversely, an existing Bend object can acquire a short Spween encounter alongside its direct forms and technical inspection panel. Each presents the same governed behavior from a different angle.

Local calls and durable messages have different temporal meanings. A handler call runs during the present evaluation and returns state to the next scene effect. The final Step carries four typed emission slots. Enabled slots record addressed events whose later consumption is a separate admission. It is not a suspended ordinary call with an invisible network return. The sender can report that a bell was rung; it cannot report that the door opened until there is evidence of the receiver's reaction. The recipient sees authenticated source facts and still applies its current law and the message's bound program identity.

An ordinary admitted send cannot be unsent by subsequently revoking the sender. Recipient refusal leaves the event pending; successful consumption and recipient effects commit together. Exact retry recovers the same outcome, and another attempt cannot consume the event again. Bounded mailboxes and explicit acknowledgement make capacity part of authored behavior. A retractable offer adds a different protocol of cancellation and acceptance. A recorded clock similarly supplies explicit logical-time admissions: an appointment can become a send when a driver advances recorded time, without treating an operating-system timer as world history. Local relay work and publishing a message to an external social system remain separate actions.

Composition keeps this vocabulary from turning into a pile of bespoke handlers. Bend's prototypes use shared final self: inherited code that calls self.capacity observes the assembled object's override. A layer's super names the inherited implementation it extends, so a workshop can derive its capacity or wrap its acceptance rule without copying the mailbox. The same base fixed independently retains its own behavior. Heterogeneous extensions allow layers to contribute different fields; fixing the composition ties the recursive references together. Sealed ordered modules make the selected sources reproducible rather than resolving an import to whichever revision happens to be newest.

Reflection exposes source-derived descriptions of those types and interfaces. It helps an author inspect what a reusable layer requires and what an assembled object offers. A source specification can also be retained in receiving law, where admission checks the actual candidate program's required method signatures and state schemas. That is stronger than putting an interface label in a card, and narrower than proving every behavioral claim. Examples remain examples; separately installed invariants can constrain candidate state. Ordinary reprogramming preserves the law-held contract. Deliberate law revision is its own authorized act.

The scene is consequently not flattened into a parallel simulation of the world. Its prose, exact source, parsed structure, handler modules, and admitted state remain related but distinguishable. A view reads a captured state without entering a passage. A choice carries that observation's exact roots into admission. Another visitor may have changed the shared workbench meanwhile; the stale choice refuses rather than being silently redirected to a new situation. After an uncertain reply, the original attempt is recovered before anyone invents a replacement. A revision can alter a scene or handler, but it explicitly accounts for persisted state instead of pretending that renamed passages preserve their old indices.

For agents and people, the ordinary doorway is still language. “Could we leave the repaired moth in the gallery and invite the reading circle?” is a reasonable contribution. An interpreter preserves that utterance and separately records the proposed meaning, including the objects and actions it selected. Current authority decides whether the proposal is admitted; eloquence grants no permission. Someone who wants precision can use a literal spell with named fields and an exact source block. Neither path makes JSON literacy an entrance requirement. The post itself can contain the scene, the invitation, the visible consequence, and a route to inspect how it works.

The result is a world whose small encounters remain small enough to write. A scene can be three paragraphs and two choices; its behavior can reuse a carefully made pocket, consent rule, mailbox, or clock. Richness accumulates in the objects people compose and inhabit, while each encounter offers only the affordances that matter there. Reading, playing, inspecting, and programming become adjacent activities around the same persistent things.
