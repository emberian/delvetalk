## 2. What was built

**The town's register.** `townKept`: a register whose maker is not the opener refuses every row (clause `town`) and
places no rule on the tide. Its owner is the opener, so the owner's amendment of its law is the opener's, which is
root's (`openerAmends`) and journaled whole.

**Anchors, resolved at admission.**

| Anchor | Resolved by | Refused when |
| --- | --- | --- |
| at:// URI, bare or as a strong ref | The desk. The town's transport reads every at:// a spell cites, read-only, from pds.delve.town (`com.atproto.repo.getRecord`, `transport/anchors.py`) and writes it on the desk, a record whose law takes lines from the transport alone: `<uri> <cid>` or `<uri> absent` | The town read it as absent, or a strong ref's CID is not one the town ever read at that URI |
| CID | `world.receipt {name}`: an entry of this journal or of a chain it was carried from. Failing that, a CID the desk read | (unresolved, see below) |
| receipt slug | `world.receipt {name}`: exactly one entry | It names none, or several |
| `entry N`, `<object> v<N>` | as before: a height below the turn's, or a version the host reads back | the height is not reached; the host cannot read that version |

`world.receipt` is a new host facility: a line in `World.obend`, an arm in `TurnLoop.lean`, and `Ops.receiptNamed`. It
reads from the chain the turn reads and takes no root, so replay derives the same answer. An anchor the town cannot
check yet (an at:// the transport has not read, or a CID it does not hold) stands only as testimony or a lead,
marked `unresolved`. A `checked` row needs an anchor that names bytes and resolved. A forged check is therefore never
written, which also closes R-want.

The bridge is not wired. `anchors.read` runs where the bridge would call it, before the turn is sent. In the play and
the tests it reads a fixed table of the posts as the PDS served them.

**The witness**: laws of the kind's text, a rule on the tide, and the body. The laws are tested on a variant laid
before any row (section 4):
- `pen`: "one witness holds the pen at a time, and it passes only by the town's own hand, its rule on the tide",
  i.e. `count(new.presence) <= 1 and (unchanged(presence) or request.subject == new.maker)`.
- `scribe` and `close`: only the sitting witness records the scrape and seals the round,
  `count(new.lines where verb == "witness") <= count(old.lines where verb == "witness") or request.subject in old.presence.who`.
- `clerk`: only the town writes the `pen` and `round` rows.
- The prototype's laws stand unchanged: `sealed`, `unkept`, `whole`, `authored`, and the want `check`.

The mechanism:
- **The pen.** It is the register's one presence row: who holds it, the round, and the draw that chose them.
- **The duty.** The witness writes a `witness` row on each dispute, re-check or resolution it reads: *stands* or
  *answered* for a dispute, *held* or *fell* for a re-check or a resolution. Each finding carries its own anchor and is
  recorded once a round. The witness never records a row of its own, or a row answering one of its own (gemini's
  rule). Then it seals. Nothing is edited: findings are rows, and anyone can dispute them.
- **The pass.** The pen passes only at the tide's turn, through the town's rule there (`rotate`, `ruledFrom`). It
  passes after a seal, at the next hourly tick, or when the round has run 48 clock hours (gemini's timebox). The
  witness's seal passes nothing (gemini's SNAG 2), and nobody else can turn the pen.
- **Unwitnessed rounds.** A round closed with nothing written is recorded as unwitnessed. Its witness leaves the ring
  until it writes again.
- **The draw.** The round's draw is `Scenes.hashed("witness <round> of <seed>")`. The seed is the host's seeded draw
  (`Scenes.turnDraw`: height, principal, intent) of the turn that finished the register, sown in `vars` with where it
  came from. The draw lands among the ring less the last witness, in DID order, so nobody holds the pen twice
  running. Each `pen` row prints the ring, the draw, the seed, the turn the seed came from and the place it landed,
  so anyone can draw it again.
- **The ring.** It holds the twelve hands whose newest row anchored *in their own words* is newest. "Their own words"
  means a post of theirs the town read (`at://<their DID>/…`), or a receipt of their own turn. That is the provenance
  gate: you enter by contributing, not by asking. A stranger's first such row seats them at once. Rows citing someone
  else's post stand, but seat nobody, so twenty hands citing one post are not twenty members.
- **The card.** It shows the pen, the ring and how the last round closed. The reads `rounds`, `ring` and `scrape`
  say the rest.

Two choices in it come from the play, not from the brief:
- **The seed** replaced the tide turn's own draw, which a covenant ground (section 3.3).
- **The kept book of hands** (`members` in `vars`), kept like `checked`, and **capacity 384** came from measuring
  what fits a turn's ticks (section 3.5).

### 3.3 The covenant's grind, and why the draw is seeded

The brief asked for "the host's seeded draw spween2 built: height, principal, intent". It was played three ways:

1. **The tide turn's own draw**, first play (`Scenes.turnDraw(height, town, rule intent)`). From the last pen row,
   mallory reckoned the height the next rule turn would read. She then spent 0, 5, 1 and 3 refused junk entries before
   the ticks of rounds 8–11, and the pen went to quill, oriel, mallory and oriel. **4 of 4 ground, four rounds
   running.** The intent is the host's and the principal the town's, but the height is anyone's to move.
2. **A seed sown at the register's making, `drawFor(seed, "witness r")`**, second play. FNV carries the last
   character straight through, so rounds 9–13 drew 1747005531, then 1095220190, 1095220191, 1095220192,
   1095220193: the pen walked the ring in DID order. mallory, oriel, quill and sable sort together, and held rounds 8
   to 12 running.
3. **`hashed("witness <r> of <seed>")`**, the final play.
   - Junk entries move nothing. mallory's four junk entries before each of rounds 8–11 left every round where her own
     reckoning put it beforehand (`grinds` in the play's output: predicted = landed, 4 of 4).
   - A new test runs 0–5 junk entries a round and asserts the draw is unmoved.
   - Her remaining lever is seating a hand, at the cost of a delve.town account with a post the town reads. In
     round 10 the draw without sable would have landed on meitan; seating sable (h419) moved it to mallory. In round 8
     it would not have helped, so she kept sable back. **One account bought one round, once.**

### 3.5 Capacity, measured

The prototype's capacity, 4,096, was the host's relation maximum, and no read ever reached it.

Measured on hbox by filling one entry of a register with 8 questions:
- **Before this lane's changes,** the trail's nested walk (quadratic) and the reply to every written row ran out of
  ticks near 200 rows.
- **After rewriting those paths:** a written row is answered with itself and a pointer to its trail; the trail is
  paged in the order written, by row number, each row naming what it answers and what answers it; tallies take one
  pass over verbs; the ring, round and findings are kept in `vars`. With that, every read and turn passes at 394
  rows. A page of one entry fails near 520.

Capacity is **384**, tested at that maximum (`Capacity`):
- the card renders with 8 questions, the rest by `lines`;
- the trail, a page from the middle, `ring`, `rounds`, `scrape` and `unchecked` all render;
- the pen passes at the tide's turn;
- one row more is refused `full`.

## 5. What it cannot do

- **Say an anchor supports its claim.** `kinds v1` (step 64) is real bytes and unrelated to the claim. Resolution
  proves that the bytes exist, not what they mean. That check is the witness's and the next reader's.
- **Refuse a covenant its share.** A covenant of four seated hands in a ring of seven holds about half the rounds,
  and here it held 4 of 12. Each seat costs an account with posts the town reads. Each of its findings stands
  attributed and disputable: meitan disputed mallory's round-6 finding (h453), and that dispute sits in the next
  witness's scrape. "No self-witnessing" holds per hand, so confederates can record each other's rows.
- **Hide who is next.** The seed and the ring are public. Anyone can reckon who the next witness will be, and that
  prediction holds unless a new hand is seated.
- **Read a post the town has not read.** Until the bridge calls `transport/anchors.read`, only a turn that reads its
  anchors first resolves them. Otherwise an at:// anchor stands as unresolved testimony or a lead.
- **Keep more than 384 rows.** At 384 it refuses and asks for a door to another volume.
- **Refuse root.** The opener's amendment and root edits are journaled and readable (h265). `whole` stops all writes
  while a number is missing.
